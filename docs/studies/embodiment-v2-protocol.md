# Two robots, one common EE policy: development and evaluation protocol

## Material Passport

- Study: `embodiment_v2_20260927`; status: development, final evaluation unopened.
- Scope: simulation-only PressButton on registered BlueROV2/Reach Alpha and custom centered RexROV2/Oberon7.
- Existing held-arm v1 outcomes and physical/success definitions remain immutable.
- Question: how do two native robot/controller systems execute a common EE target policy with arm motion?

## Gates and conditions

1. Audit v1 traces, including all five success predicates and matched TCP reset displacements. The observed Blue bottleneck is insufficient button travel, not loss of attitude or alignment. Existing data do not identify a unique causal contribution of PID versus contact geometry.
2. Check Rex moving-arm dynamics. The nominal held-arm closure omits pose-dependent rigid inertia and moving-arm reaction. An opt-in closure uses the same native masses/base added mass and RNEA articulated rigid reaction. Joint acceleration and contact force are measured one physics step late; arm added mass remains unmodeled. Native coefficients, collisions, spring, damping, joint limits and motor limits are unchanged. CPU held-joint equivalence and reaction identities precede simulation. Run no-contact motion at 240/480 Hz; require finite states/wrenches, native motor limits, return to stationary operation, joint rate limits and agreement over the scored motion window (RMS TCP difference <=10 mm, attitude difference <=0.5 deg, joint difference <=0.02 rad). Failure prevents moving-arm final evaluation.
3. Develop one shared rate-limited weighted damped IK controller: world translation of a level base plus native arm joints. The same scripted EE approach and target orientation feed both adapters. Each robot keeps its native station-keeping/actuator gains. This compares complete systems, not isolated morphology or learned transfer.
4. Development reset IDs: 85000–85002. Additional diagnostic IDs must be documented separately. Final IDs reserved: 86000–86029; do not run or inspect them before numerical/contact gates and a source/config freeze. No neural training or training-seed repeats.

## Reference and outcomes

Begin development with the v1 fixture at (3,0,1.1) m, TCP standoff 0.12 m, 5 s settling and 0.10 m approach over 8 s, 20 s total. Any reference adjustment must be justified from development contact geometry and recorded before final freeze. Robot sizes and task assets are not rescaled. The task uses the original 4 mm travel, distance <0.13 m, alignment >0.70, base attitude <0.25 rad and angular speed <0.35 rad/s, held for four control samples at 30 Hz. Success is independently replayed from raw traces. Numerical convergence checks retain both successes and failures.

Record target/measured TCP, base position/attitude, joint targets/states, motor commands/forces, plunger and housing contacts where available, all success predicates and timing. Report tracking by approach/contact phase as well as whole episode. A no-command control has zero motor commands and fixed initial arm targets; it is not a fair test of an actively moving arm without motors.

Deliverables after gates: 30 paired resets per robot, source-bound report, interpretable tracking/attitude/motor plots and synchronized representative video. Keep these expert/system results separate from learned ACT/DP rows. Show original v1 as historical; do not replace its 0/30 and 30/30 with new numbers.

## Development notes

- The first Blue IK probe requested identity orientation in the raw gripper-link frame. CAD FK shows an intrinsic approximately 1.94 rad roll at the nominal posture; this drove the wrist command beyond the actuator-interface range (raw normalized maximum 3.53). That probe is retained as a rejected adapter check, not a physical robot result. The adapter now declares a fixed intrinsic-roll frame calibration and enforces the existing action bounds inside IK. This does not move CAD, change joint limits or relax the success orientation predicate.
- At the original 0.10 m stroke, Blue's recorded spring travel is below 4 mm. A calibrated-frame 0.112 m development stroke reaches about 3.77 mm, without housing contact and without action clipping. A 0.12 m stroke places the common commanded TCP at the fixture origin; this is the next physical reference check, with all task parameters unchanged. These are development references, not a final-seed search.
- Rex base composite inertia calculated from the source URDF matches the actual imported PhysX generalized base block within 0.00023 in matrix entries at reset. This supports the inertial frame/mass mapping, not arbitrary moving-arm accuracy.
- No-contact Rex 240/480 Hz gate passed: TCP RMS difference 6.268 mm, maximum attitude difference 0.1813 degrees, maximum joint difference 0.000211 rad. All native motor limits held. This covers only the specified small motion.
- Absolute IK references (`actual + correction`) left a persistent Rex height deficit of about 133 mm with shoulder target tracking error about 0.071 rad. The shared controller now integrates the resolved-rate correction at unit gain (1/s), bounded by native joint rates and 0.1 m/s base-reference rate. Native inner-loop gains remain unchanged. At the fixture-origin goal (0.12 m approach), both platforms pass 3/3 development resets; both no-command controls fail. Blue references stay within native action bounds. Rex's 1,800 recorded powered poses pass the nonadjacent-link CAD collision-hull audit.
- Contact timestep checks use those same development resets and controller at 240/480 Hz, before final evaluation. Housing contact is measured, not silently excluded. A separate demonstration will use preselected development reset 85000, with live physics and a camera-only wrapper; its results will not enter the 30-reset score.
- Unbounded integration preserved development successes at 240/480 Hz but failed trajectory convergence (Rex TCP difference 11.603 mm, attitude 1.377 deg; Blue attitude 3.216 deg). Base-reference tracking error grew to 0.26 m (Rex) and 0.13 m (Blue) while the button was at its 9 mm hard stop. The controller now conditionally stops integration farther into an axis whose reference leads the measured coordinate by half a second of its declared speed; reversing the correction is still allowed. This shared anti-windup rule precedes final testing.
- Blue's old rate-check runner also kept a two-step motor delay, inadvertently halving the delay in seconds at 480 Hz. Subsequent checks preserve the nominal 2/240-second delay by scaling its integer step count with physics rate. The 240 Hz physical configuration is unchanged. The earlier Blue check is retained as confounded diagnostic evidence, not a pure timestep comparison.
- The anti-windup Blue 240/480 Hz pair still fails the trajectory gate despite success invariance (TCP RMS 3.618 mm, maximum attitude difference 2.367 degrees, joint difference 0.0213 rad). A 960 Hz development check will determine whether 480 Hz is sufficient. Final integration uses the lowest common tested rate that passes the predeclared tolerances against the next finer rate; no tolerance is relaxed. Additional numerical checks are not new learned-policy experiments.
- Further refinement did not fix the contact branch: Blue 480/960 Hz still fails (attitude difference 3.807 deg), whereas before contact (<11 s) its maximum attitude difference is 0.231 deg and joint difference 0.00144 rad. First >0.5 deg divergence occurs at 12.867 s with approximately 2 mm button travel. Rex bounded 240/480 also fails (0.934 deg, 0.04357 rad joint difference). Further timestep refinement is stopped; success invariance alone does not pass the quantitative gate.
- The shared controller now switches at measured plunger normal force >0.5 N from free-space integration to an absolute IK correction plus frozen load-compensation bias. The bias is chosen for a bumpless transition (`reference - actual - IK correction`). This contact latch is a controller condition, not the physical success criterion; both systems use the same rule and force threshold. It prevents further accumulation against the object while preserving the precontact compensation for arm weight. The test remains a scripted state-feedback EE policy, not a learned policy or an open-loop Cartesian trajectory.
- A second timing confound was found in the Blue runner: its hydrodynamic acceleration filter retained per-step alpha=0.2 at every physics rate. At 240/480/960 Hz this changes the filter time constant from 18.67 to 9.34 to 4.67 ms. This is not pure integrator refinement. Subsequent runs preserve the original 240 Hz time constant using `alpha(dt)=1-0.8**(dt*240)`. The original 240 Hz model is unchanged; no native benchmark defaults are edited. Earlier Blue convergence failures cannot be attributed uniquely to the IK/contact controller. The contact-latch 960 Hz diagnostic was stopped once this confound was established. Rate auditing now checks both motor delay and acceleration-filter time constants explicitly.
- Every-step instrumentation of the legacy Blue hydrodynamics established near-continuous acceleration limiting: 4,787/4,800 physics steps in each powered development episode (peak unclipped acceleration/limit ratio about 1.674). Neither force nor torque clipping activated. The generic legacy arm rule assigns rotational added inertia up to 0.02 kg m² even to small fingers and bookkeeping frames. The existing `geometry_scaled_arm_hydrodynamics` variant, already used for grasp tasks, scales rotational coefficients with link dimensions and excludes internal/tool bookkeeping frames from independent wetted-body loading. This is now an explicit **opt-in physical-model variant** for development, not an edit of legacy PressButton physics or a silent replacement of v1. Success criteria, masses, actuators and motor limits remain unchanged. It must pass numerical/limiter checks before any new final study. Its coefficients are engineering estimates, not hardware calibration.
- The geometry-scaled Blue model removes all recorded acceleration limiting (0/4,800 steps, versus 4,787 legacy steps) on all three resets. TCP timestep RMS difference improves to 1.035 mm. The first-touch hold, however, leaves pressing near the 4 mm threshold (3/3 at 240 Hz, 0/3 at 480 Hz), and attitude still fails the 0.5-degree gate. This controller stopped accumulating pressing load before the requested task progress was delivered. The common script now latches compensation at **6 mm measured button travel**, between the unchanged 4 mm success threshold and the 9 mm physical end stop. This is a controller progress target, not a success relaxation. Final trials still require the original sustained alignment/stability predicates. The original first-touch diagnostic is preserved via `--hold-travel 0`; the new candidate uses `--hold-travel .006` explicitly on both robots.
- The final common physics rate must be explicitly present in both robots' passed adjacent-rate comparisons. If one passes 240/480 Hz and the other 480/960 Hz, use the shared validated 480 Hz, without requiring redundant checks. All numerical tolerances remain unchanged. For the geometry-scaled Blue variant, require zero powered acceleration/force/torque limiting over the recorded physics-step audit in development and final trials. Both candidate comparisons must also match the hold-progress trigger and physical-model variant.
- Measured-state redundant IK also amplifies small simulation differences into different compensating joint paths. In the Rex 480/960 Hz first-touch pair, a 0.0512 rad joint difference at 6.567 s corresponds to only 0.0985 degrees of local tool orientation difference, with compensating base/arm motion. The numeric joint gate still fails and is not waived.
- The new `--ik-mode reference` candidate integrates the same weighted IK in a virtual, URDF-based reference state. Native joint references are independent of measured joint-tracking errors; measured tool-to-base displacement adjusts the base command to compensate real arm deflection. Both command rates and native arm limits are retained. At 6 mm progress it holds the last native base and joint setpoints. This replaces measured-state IK for the candidate; it is not another physics change. Virtual reference FK was independently compared with 300 recorded simulator poses per robot: maximum TCP disagreement 2.33 micrometres (Blue) and 4.63 micrometres (Rex).
- The trace distinguishes the common scripted TCP goal (`target_tool`) from the internal kinematic reference (`planned_tool`) and actual native base setpoint. Goal error includes finite controller response and task-progress holding; it must not be described as isolated IK solver error.
- Reference-state IK reduces Blue 240/480 joint difference to 0.000449 rad and attitude difference to 0.1047 degrees, without clipping; success is still marginal because the original fixture-origin command reaches only approximately 3.85 mm at 480 Hz. Rex joint difference falls to 0.00464 rad and attitude difference to 0.3286 degrees, but TCP RMS difference is 10.399 mm, narrowly failing the unchanged 10 mm gate.
- The next common reference adds **20 mm commanded preload beyond the fixture origin**, giving a 0.14 m total scripted approach. This compensates observed native position-loop compliance; it is a target command, not geometric penetration or a changed spring. Both robots retain the 6 mm progress hold, native force limits and the original 4 mm success criterion. The candidate is checked at 480/960 Hz; no final states are opened and no gate is relaxed.
- The reference-state, 0.14 m approach Rex candidate passes 480/960 Hz: TCP RMS 6.363 mm, attitude maximum 0.2238 degrees, joint maximum 0.00362 rad, with 3/3 successes and failed motors-off controls at both rates. This Rex configuration is retained.
- Blue's corresponding pair preserves 3/3 success but narrowly fails attitude agreement (0.5987 degrees), with 7–10 transient acceleration-limit events at 960 Hz. A generalized inertial audit over 40 recorded postures gives maximum eigenvalue of `M_rigid^-1 M_added` approximately 1764.03 for legacy coefficients and 1.759 for geometry-scaled coefficients. In the isolated linear delayed-acceleration recurrence, stability requires `alpha < 2/(1+lambda_max)`; the native alpha=0.2 violates the legacy bound (0.00113) and satisfies the scaled bound (0.7249). This is not a proof for nonlinear contact/drives.
- The scaled candidate now explicitly tests `--acceleration-cap none`: removal of the old numerical acceleration clipping, justified by the inertial correction and linear audit. Motor/joint limits and force/torque safeguards remain. Every-step pre-sanitization finiteness is checked, and crossings of the old acceleration thresholds are recorded separately. Final acceptance still requires the unchanged trajectory tolerances and no applied force/torque clipping; old source-model defaults remain unchanged.

The inherited probe counted continuous physics steps over 4/30 s; all retained development outcomes already agree with the common 30 Hz replay.
